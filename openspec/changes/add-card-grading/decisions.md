## Goals

- A collector knows the fee, the level and the return date before the visit, pays once at the counter after every card is checked, and is told the moment the grades are in
- A dealer pastes a list, takes the Bulk level and the longer visit, and collects everything at once
- A collector whose card came back worth more than the level knows the difference the day the grades post, never at the counter
- A collector reads one status and whose move it is on every submission page, and every exception as a fact on one card with the money it changes
- A collector, or one person they name, collects in person with the pickup code; a slab can go into a vault case from the same counter
- Shop staff work a queue cut by what each submission waits for, hand cards in and back through a runbook, and ship and receive by the batch
- The grader gets one order at one level, one invoice and one shipment back per batch, and its words reach the collector unchanged
- Every default the pages run on is a setting the console reads, adopted from the canvas until its owner confirms it

## Non-Goals

- Shipping slabs back; the collector or the person they name collects in person
- CGC's and BGS's fee figures; their sheets are Commercial's, and the levels exist as data
- A vault case carrying the grade and the cert as valuation fields; the follow-on `carry-grade-into-vault-case`
- An auction consignment from the graded record; a follow-on
- Online payment, a deposit or a card hold at booking; every amount is taken at the till
- Staff notifications; the queue's badges, tiles and day strip are the signal
- A grader API integration; the morning check is a person reading the grader's order status and typing the stage, and a follow-on names the provider
- A second shop; the picker shows more as they open, and the first release has one
- Goodwill on the fee for a card returned ungraded, unless Commercial asks for it
- A change to the diary's own logic; grading books three catalogue entries over the per-product entrypoint

## Decisions

| Q | Asked | Decided | Instead of |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | One change, or one per surface? | One change, `add-card-grading`, with nine new capabilities and one delta on the vault's retention | A change per surface: the plan, the counter and the console share one status machine, and three deltas on it would revert each other at archive |
| Q2 | Can a submission mix graders or levels? | One submission is one grader and one level; a card that needs another goes in a second submission on the same drop-off | A level per card in one submission: the batch is one grader and one level, so a mixed submission could not be invoiced or shipped as one |
| Q3 | Is a refused, held or lost card a status? | A card's outcome is a fact on the card, never a status of the submission; the rest of the cards carry on | A status per exception: ten statuses would become dozens, and four cards with one returned ungraded would have no status to read |
| Q4 | When is the fee taken? | Nothing is paid before every card is checked and the agreement sealed; the till opens on the seal | Paying online at booking, or at the counter before the check: the collector would pay for a card refused minutes later |
| Q5 | What does the fee do on an ungraded, a refused and a withdrawn card? | ❓ commercial - recommended: the fee stands on a card the grader returns ungraded, a card refused at the counter is never charged, a card withdrawn before its batch closes is refunded at the till; told on the review step and in clause 3 | Goodwill on the ungraded fee: the grader charges it either way and the collector is warned twice, so the design advises against |
| Q6 | Who carries the grader's upcharge? | ❓ commercial - recommended: Grade10 fronts the difference on the grader's invoice and collects it at the counter before hand-back, with no cap and no pre-authorisation; a collector who never collects leaves it secured on the card and recovered through the storage ladder | A pre-authorisation at hand-in, or a cap on the upcharge: the review step already names each card's possible upcharge and what the higher level costs now |
| Q7 | Are slabs shipped back? | ❓ pm - recommended: not in the first release; the pages say the collector or a person they name collects | Shipping inside Hong Kong at cost, to the address on the agreement, from the signed-in account: it needs transit cover that does not exist, and is a second release if wanted |
| Q8 | Where do the defaults live? | Every default is a setting the console reads, seeded from the canvas, never a constant | Constants in code with a deploy per change: every value is one an owner has yet to confirm |
| Q9 | Which clock does a day run on? | A day is the brand's, `Asia/Hong_Kong`, for the cut-off, a due date, a reminder day and the queue's day; an instant is UTC on the wire and in the database | Shop-local timestamps stored as such, which the vault already refused |
| Q10 | Does grading check identity? | Grading needs no identity record; above the threshold the counter glances at an ID matching the name and keeps nothing, and the vault's identity duties do not apply | The vault's identity check at hand-back: a KYC duty grading has no reason to carry, and a photograph nobody needs to keep |
| Q11 | Can staff release the cards to somebody else? | ❓ ops - recommended: the counter releases to nobody but the collector or the person named on the page, code or no code, and staff have no override | A staff override with a reason: the collector renames the person from their phone in the same minute, so the override would only ever bypass them |
| Q12 | Where is the ID glance threshold? | ❓ ops - recommended: HKD 10,000 declared in total | Lower, which slows every pickup; higher, which releases a HKD 15,000 slab on a forwarded email |
| Q13 | Which channels, which languages? | Email only, English; the console's WhatsApp templates are click-to-chat, pressed by staff | SMS or WhatsApp automation, and a second language: the vault's documents and messages are English, and the screen's chrome already speaks the collector's language |
| Q14 | Who emails about the drop-off? | The diary sends nothing for a product booking; grading sends its own booked, moved, cancelled, missed and day-before messages, each carrying the list and the fee | The diary's own booking mails: they carry no list, no fee and no batch day, and the vault's visit already works this way |
| Q15 | How are the documents signed? | The submission agreement and the hand-back receipt ride the vault's ceremony with an explicit "no identity required" packet, decided per template and never a null reference; the intake receipt is issued, not signed; the doc-sign host contract gains the option, which is engineering's in `tech-design.md` | A grading-owned signature or paper: two seals for one shop, and a receipt for money already taken needs no signature |
| Q16 | How is money taken? | At the till, one Grading Service POS line per card at the level's fee, written back to the submission by reference; an upcharge, a storage fee and a refund are lines the same way; no online payment | Stripe at booking: the fee is not known until the cards are checked, and a refund would then leave the till |
| Q17 | What does the shop track with the grader? | The batch, one grader and one level, closed Thursday 19:00 and shipped Friday: it is what the grader invoices and ships back | Tracking per submission: seven submissions in one box would carry seven tracking numbers for one parcel |
| Q18 | Does `grade10-site/appointment/booking` change? | No delta: the collector's booking surface lists customer-bookable services only and already names the Grading visit the walk-in books; the product-bound drop-off is never listed there, and is data the diary already takes, a service bound to a product with a Bulk variant | A delta adding grading's drop-off to the public flow: a product-bound service is never customer bookable, and a second delta on an in-flight capability would revert whichever archived first |
| Q19 | Does the scheduling vocabulary know `grading`? | ❓ tech - recommended: the `Product` row of `shared/appointment/scheduling` reads `vault`, `finance`, or none; `grading` joins it as one word in `add-multi-store-appointments`'s own delta, extended rather than doubled | A delta on that capability from this change: it is still in flight, and whichever change archived second would revert the first |
| Q20 | Where are grading's records retained? | ❓ legal - recommended: the agreement, the receipts and the intake and hand-back photographs join the vault's retention table at its windows, 2,555 days, and grading holds no identity class; one review and one erasure path serve both products, as one delta on `grade10-site/vault/retention-and-erasure` | A retention table of grading's own: two reviews, two erasure paths, and a second window for the same kind of paper |
| Q21 | Does `grade10-site/vault/documents-and-signing` change? | No delta: the vault's packets keep their identity duty; grading's packet, naming the person from the booking with no identity record, is grading's own requirement in `counter-documents` | Loosening the vault's packet rule for both products: the vault's agreement must name the person the shop checked |
| Q22 | Does `grade10-site/vault/case-intake` change? | No delta: a slab vaulted at the counter opens an ordinary vault case on the collector's phone, under an account, with its photographs; the grade and the cert as valuation fields are the follow-on | A case opened from the submission with the grade carried in: it moves the vault's intake and valuation, which this change leaves alone |
| Q23 | The clocks? | ❓ ops - recommended: `grading.plan_nudge_days` 21 and `grading.plan_expiry_days` 30 for a plan with no drop-off booked; `grading.reminder_days` 30 and 60, `grading.storage_from_day` 90, `grading.notice_day` 180 for cards not collected; `grading.batch_cutoff` Thursday 19:00 with the batch shipping Friday, checked against the courier's pickup schedule | A shorter expiry, which loses lists collectors meant to book; a cut-off the courier cannot meet |
| Q24 | Settlement for a card not returned or damaged? | ❓ ops - recommended: declared value plus the fee within `grading.settlement_days` 14 days of the batch being received, as a POS refund to the card paid with, else by bank transfer; Grade10 claims from the grader or the courier itself | A shorter window, which fronts every claim before the grader answers; a longer one, which moves the wait and the dispute onto the collector |
| Q25 | The storage fee? | ❓ commercial - recommended: `grading.storage_fee_per_card_month` HKD 30 from day 90, due before collection, a nudge and not revenue; vault storage stays free, so a slab kept on purpose moves into a case | A higher fee as revenue, or none: none leaves the safe full, and revenue makes the shop a paid store with no policy behind it |
| Q26 | Notice and disposal for uncollected cards? | ❓ legal - recommended: reminders at 30 and 60 days, the fee at 90, written notice at 180 by registered post to the address taken at signing and by email, giving 30 days, then disposal under the Disposal of Uncollected Goods Ordinance (Cap. 456) with the proceeds less fees held for the collector; clause 6 carries it, and Legal words the notice and the clause together | Email alone as the notice, or no disposal: a card nobody collects would sit in the safe forever |
| Q27 | The fee sheet? | ❓ commercial - recommended: PSA's sheet as drawn stands as example figures, one sheet per grader and level with its ceiling, fee, cover line and estimate; `grading.cover_pct` 1.5% of declared value at Express and Super Express; turnarounds quoted door to door from the ship day, the grader's published time plus two weeks | A flat fee at every level: it under-recovers where cover scales with value |
| Q28 | The Bulk cap and its slot? | ❓ ops - recommended: `grading.max_cards_bulk` 100 cards a submission at Bulk from a pasted list, 20 at every other level; the Bulk drop-off is the longer diary service, about 45 minutes; above the cap, a second submission on another day | No cap: checking a card takes 20 to 30 seconds, and a 300-card list would hold the desk all afternoon |
| Q29 | Cover for the cards in the shop and in transit? | ❓ commercial - recommended: `grading.safe_declared_cap` HKD 300,000 of declared value held in the safe, an operational cap until cover is bought; before launch a goods-in-trust quote spanning grading and the vault, transit insurance for outbound batches, and the courier's collectibles terms in writing; clause 5 changes the day a policy is bought | Launching on the courier's declared-value cover alone: couriers cap collectibles near USD 1,000 a parcel, and clause 5 promises settlement at declared value |
| Q30 | Which diary services? | ❓ pm - recommended: three catalogue entries, no new diary logic: the product-bound Grading drop-off at about 20 minutes, its Bulk variant at about 45, and the customer-bookable Grading visit kept for the walk-in without a list; the names and durations | One service with a duration per booking: the diary sizes a slot by the service, and a Bulk list needs the longer one |
| Q31 | How many shops? | One; the picker shows more as they open | Building the picker for several: the diary already knows shops, and nothing else changes |
| Q32 | Does a plan need an account? | No; the plan lives under the email given, its link is emailed the moment the collector leaves and opens on any device, and signing in with the same email, no password, lists every submission | An account before the wizard: the vault's collector pages already open from an emailed link |
| Q33 | Does a plan lapse? | ❓ ops - recommended: nudged at 21 days and expired at 30 with no drop-off booked, told by a short email; prices and references move, so an old list is not kept | Keeping a plan forever: the fee sheet and the reference sales behind it would be stale by the visit |
| Q34 | How do the grader's stages reach the page? | A person reads the grader's order status most mornings and types the stage; every stage lands on the timeline in the grader's words, and the grader is an outside party acting under its own terms | A grader API integration: a follow-on names the provider once one exists |
| Q35 | Are staff notified? | No; the queue's badges, tiles and day strip are the signal, and every badge is derived at the read | Emails to staff: the vault's queue already works as the inbox |
| Q36 | Does a `planned` submission show on the queue? | ❓ pm - recommended: it stays off the queue until booked; the collector's own list holds it | A Drafts view: the queue is cut by what the shop waits for, and a draft waits for nobody in the shop |
| Q37 | Where do the blocks live? | The collector-facing blocks in the store's `packages/ui` as `shared/ui/grading-submission` with stories; the console views in the monorepo's `packages/grading/admin-frontend`, composing the console blocks there | A `shared/ui/grading-console` capability: `packages/ui` carries no console blocks, and the seams report places them in the monorepo |
| Q38 | What happens when the grader keeps one card back? | The submission stays ready for the rest; the receipt names the card still out, and a second hand-back closes it | Holding the whole submission until the last card returns: three collectors' slabs would wait on one card |
| Q39 | Is running late a status? | No; it is the estimate against the clock, read on the page and by the queue the same way, and a re-estimate emails every collector in the batch | A `late` status: it would be written by a sweep and unwritten by a re-estimate, for a fact the clock already tells |
| Q40 | What if a collector does not accept a grade? | The grade is the grader's decision; a review is a new submission at the grader's review fee, asked for at the counter | A review action on the submission: it reopens a closed batch for a service the grader prices itself |
| Q41 | What does the counter keep of the ID glance? | Nothing; the receipt records that an ID was matched to the name and nothing kept | A photograph or the document number: it would make grading an identity holder and give it the vault's retention duty |
