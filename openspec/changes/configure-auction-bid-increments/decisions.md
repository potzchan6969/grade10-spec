## Goals

- Collectors bid on steps that match Goldin's published schedule
- Retuning an increment is a configuration change, with no change to the spec
- The manual shows the values in force

## Non-Goals

- An operator screen or an API to edit the schedule
- A schedule per listing, per campaign or per bidder
- A currency other than USD, or a second schedule
- Moving the bid ceiling, the opening-price rule or the verified-bidder threshold
- Re-pricing a bid that already stands
- Defining Goldin's extended bidding, buyer's premium or other FAQ rules

## Decisions

| Q | Asked | Decided | Instead of |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | Where does the schedule live, and who changes it? | A versioned configuration file, changed by pull request and deploy - decided by the round (recommended) | A database setting edited in the admin app: it adds a table, a screen, an audit trail and a fetch on every client, for a schedule that changes rarely. An environment variable: it has no review and no history |
| Q2 | What happens to bids already standing when the values change? | The next bid reads the schedule afresh from the amount being beaten, and standing bids keep their amount - decided by the round (recommended) | Freezing the schedule on each listing when it opens: it contradicts the rule that no listing keeps its own increment, and adds state per listing |
| Q3 | What is the increment below $10, where Goldin names none? | $1, so the opening price on a $0 start stays $1 - decided by the round (recommended) | $2, extending Goldin's first tier down to $0: it also moves the opening price on a $0 start |
| Q4 | Which values does the schedule start with? | Goldin's published schedule from [How does bidding work?](https://goldin.co/faq), eighteen tiers from $10 to $1,500,000 and up | The current six tiers: they sit below Goldin's at every price above $100, so the same lot steps differently on the two sites |
| Q5 | Do the values stay in the requirement? | No: the requirement carries the rule, and the manual shows the values in force | Keeping the table in the spec: every retune would need a delta and an acceptance, which is what this change removes |
| Q6 | Does the change reach lots already open? | Yes, on the next bid; a lot open at deploy takes the new minimum - decided by the round | Applying it to lots scheduled after deploy: two schedules would run at once, and a collector could not tell which one a lot follows |
| Q7 | When a collector's page is stale after a schedule change (an open page's Min, a bid at the old minimum, a maximum below the minimum or below the starting price), what happens? | The page's Min refreshes on its next read or bid attempt, not live; a bid or maximum below the minimum the server now holds is refused, names that minimum, and records nothing; a first maximum must reach the opening price - decided by the round. Source: [Bidding · Refusals](/p/grade10-site/auction/bidding#refusals), "Below a next minimum that moved after the page showed it" and "A refused bid is not a bid", with the first-maximum line under Bid Increments and the 450-against-400 row of Auto-Bidding, and Q6 (the change reaches an open lot on its next bid) | Accepting the bid at the old minimum because the page showed it: it contradicts the Refusals table. A live push of the new Min to open pages: more than a task group, and no page line asks for it |
| Q8 | Does a standing maximum read the changed schedule when it later answers a rival bid? | Yes: every proxy step reads the schedule afresh from the amount being beaten; a standing maximum keeps its amount and the earlier resolutions stand - decided by the round. Source: Q2 and the requirement that every tier increment is read from the schedule in force when the minimum is calculated | Reading the schedule in force when the maximum was committed: it needs a schedule version stored per maximum, which Q2 ruled out |
| Q9 | May a bid or a tier boundary carry a fraction of a dollar? | Amounts are whole minor units of USD, so a bid may carry cents; a tier includes its lower bound at the exact minor unit, and a price one minor unit below a bound is in the lower tier - decided by the round. Source: [Money Amounts · Values](/p/platform/shared/money-amounts#values), "An integer count of minor units", the auction's bid form refusal of an amount that is not a whole count of minor units, and [Bidding · Bid Increments](/p/grade10-site/auction/bidding#bid-increments), "A tier includes its lower bound" | Whole dollars only: it adds a rule no page states and moves the bid form's validity rule |
| Q10 | Do Min, Highest bid and the bid field read USD only when a collector views Grade10 in another display currency? | USD only on every bid surface; a USD fact becomes HKD only at settlement - decided by the round. Source: [Bidding · Currencies](/p/grade10-site/auction/bidding#currencies), "One unit for every lot" and "Settlement currency" | A converted reading beside the USD amount: no page asks for a converted bid surface |
| Q11 | Does a house maximum, or a bid placed in extended bidding, read the same schedule as a collector's bid? | Yes: one lookup on the amount being beaten for every bidder and every bid - decided by the round. Source: the PRD's Increments and Resolve decision rows, "one USD schedule for every lot" and "no listing-level override", and "A bid Grade10 places is a bid" | A separate house schedule: a second schedule contradicts the Increments decision |
| Q12 | What happens when the configured schedule is invalid: no tier from $0, unsorted or overlapping tiers, a zero or negative increment, an empty list? | ❓ product manager - recommended: A. Options: A - the file fails when it loads, so its tests and the start of the Worker and the site fail and a bad file never ships; the last deployed schedule stays in force. B - it loads, and bids are refused until it is fixed. No page or durable spec states either; `tech-design.md` writes A | B: a collector cannot bid until an engineer ships a fix |
| Q13 | Is the $1 floor a fixed rule, or only the schedule's starting value? | ❓ product manager - recommended: A. Options: A - the floor tier is configuration like any other: the opening minimum on a $0 start is the lowest configured increment, and `spec.md` and `tech-design.md` stop stating 100 minor units for it. B - the $1 floor is fixed in the requirement, as `spec.md` and the validator in `tech-design.md` now write it, and a retune of it needs a delta. The two readings differ: QA1's opening-minimum case follows the lowest configured increment, Dev's requirement, scenario SC-12 and validator fix it at 100, and the goal "retuning an increment is a configuration change, with no change to the spec" points to A | B: the first tier cannot be retuned without a spec change, against the first goal |
| Q14 | Do `auto-bidding`, `bidding-history`, `auction`, `account-record` and `grade10-admin/auction/internal-bid` get a MODIFIED delta in this change, because their scenarios and worked examples quote amounts that follow the old six tiers? | ❓ product manager - recommended: A. Options: A - give each a MODIFIED delta that restates the amounts so they hold under any values; the proposal's Modified Capabilities grows from one to five, and acceptance waits on five more deltas. B - leave them as illustrative given values and rewrite only the tests and fixtures in grade10; the durable specs then state amounts the configured values contradict. No behaviour changes either way; it moves scope | B: the durable specs quote amounts the configuration contradicts |
