## Goals

- Collectors bid on steps that match Goldin's published schedule
- Retuning an increment is a configuration change, with no change to the spec
- The manual shows the values in force

## Non-Goals

- An operator screen or an API to edit the schedule
- A schedule per listing, per campaign or per bidder
- A currency other than USD, or a second schedule
- Moving the bid ceiling, the opening-price rule or the verified-bidder threshold
- Re-pricing a bid that already stands
- Bids, maxima, starting prices, tier bounds or increments in cents (Q9, Q16)
- Rewriting, rounding or migrating a starting price already stored with cents (Q18)
- Defining Goldin's extended bidding, buyer's premium or other FAQ rules
- A check of the configured values at load or at run time: a test in CI holds the file valid (Q12)

## Decisions

| Q | Asked | Decided | Instead of |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | Where does the schedule live, and who changes it? | A versioned configuration file, changed by pull request and deploy - decided by the round (recommended) | A database setting edited in the admin app: it adds a table, a screen, an audit trail and a fetch on every client, for a schedule that changes rarely. An environment variable: it has no review and no history |
| Q2 | What happens to bids already standing when the values change? | The next bid reads the schedule afresh from the amount being beaten, and standing bids keep their amount - decided by the round (recommended) | Freezing the schedule on each listing when it opens: it contradicts the rule that no listing keeps its own increment, and adds state per listing |
| Q3 | What is the increment below $10, where Goldin names none? | $1, so the opening price on a $0 start stays $1 - decided by the round (recommended) | $2, extending Goldin's first tier down to $0: it also moves the opening price on a $0 start |
| Q4 | Which values does the schedule start with? | Goldin's published schedule from [How does bidding work?](https://goldin.co/faq), eighteen tiers from $10 to $1,500,000 and up | The current six tiers: they sit below Goldin's at every price above $100, so the same lot steps differently on the two sites |
| Q5 | Do the values stay in the requirement? | No: the requirement carries the rule, and the manual shows the values in force | Keeping the table in the spec: every retune would need a delta and an acceptance, which is what this change removes |
| Q6 | Does the change reach lots already open? | Yes, on the next bid; a lot open at deploy takes the new minimum - decided by the round | Applying it to lots scheduled after deploy: two schedules would run at once, and a collector could not tell which one a lot follows |
| Q7 | When a collector's page is stale after a schedule change (an open page's Min, a bid at the old minimum, a maximum below the minimum or below the starting price), what happens? | The page's Min refreshes on its next read or bid attempt, not live; a bid or maximum below the minimum the server now holds is refused, names that minimum, and records nothing; a first maximum must reach the opening price - decided by the round. Source: [Bidding · Refusals](/p/grade10-site/auction/bidding#refusals), "Below a next minimum that moved after the page showed it" and "A refused bid is not a bid", with the first-maximum line under Bid Increments and the 450-against-400 row of Auto-Bidding, and Q6 (the change reaches an open lot on its next bid) | Accepting the bid at the old minimum because the page showed it: it contradicts the Refusals table. A live push of the new Min to open pages: more than a task group, and no page line asks for it |
| Q8 | Does a standing maximum read the changed schedule when it later answers a rival bid? | Yes: every proxy step reads the schedule afresh from the amount being beaten; a standing maximum keeps its amount and the earlier resolutions stand - decided by the round. Source: Q2 and the requirement that every tier increment is read from the schedule in force when the minimum is calculated | Reading the schedule in force when the maximum was committed: it needs a schedule version stored per maximum, which Q2 ruled out |
| Q9 | May a bid, a maximum or a configured tier bound or increment carry a fraction of a dollar? | The product manager's own answer, in their words: "we shall accept whole dollar bid and increment configs only". Grade10 accepts only whole-dollar bids and maxima, and the configured schedule holds whole-dollar tier bounds and whole-dollar increments only. Amounts stay stored as integer minor units, so a whole dollar is a multiple of one major unit, taken from the USD exponent of `@grade10/utils/money`. A bid or maximum with cents is refused, naming the rule, and nothing moves. Source: the product manager's answer. It matches the bid form, whose custom field already takes whole USD units only ([Bidding · Auction Panel](/p/grade10-site/auction/bidding#auction-panel), "Custom maximum") | Whole minor units with cents allowed, the round's recommended (a): a bid could carry cents, and a price one minor unit below a bound would sit in the lower tier. It lost because the product manager wants every bid and every step in whole dollars, and the bid form never lets a collector type cents, so a cents rule would state a path no collector can walk |
| Q10 | Do Min, Highest bid and the bid field read USD only when a collector views Grade10 in another display currency? | USD only on every bid surface; a USD fact becomes HKD only at settlement - decided by the round. Source: [Bidding · Currencies](/p/grade10-site/auction/bidding#currencies), "One unit for every lot" and "Settlement currency" | A converted reading beside the USD amount: no page asks for a converted bid surface |
| Q11 | Does a house maximum, or a bid placed in extended bidding, read the same schedule as a collector's bid? | Yes: one lookup on the amount being beaten for every bidder and every bid - decided by the round. Source: the PRD's Increments and Resolve decision rows, "one USD schedule for every lot" and "no listing-level override", and "A bid Grade10 places is a bid" | A separate house schedule: a second schedule contradicts the Increments decision |
| Q12 | What happens when the configured schedule is invalid: no tier from $0, unsorted or overlapping tiers, a zero or negative increment, an empty list, or a last tier with an upper bound? | The product manager's own answer, in their words: "write a test to ensure config file is valid". A test that runs in CI holds the file valid: a tier from 0, tiers sorted ascending, no overlapping or duplicate lower bounds, whole-dollar tier bounds, positive whole-dollar increments (Q9), a non-empty list and an unbounded last tier. Nothing refuses a bad file at run time, and the spec states only the rule, not how it is enforced | A validator that throws when the file loads, so the Worker and the site fail to start (the round's recommended A). Bids refused until an engineer ships a fix (B): a collector cannot bid while the file is bad |
| Q13 | Is the $1 floor a fixed rule, or only the schedule's starting value? | The product manager's own answer: configurable like the other tiers. The lowest tier is the first row of the configuration, and the opening minimum on a 0 start is the lowest configured increment. No requirement, scenario, case or design line fixes it at 100 minor units; the PRD may keep $1 as the current value. The wording patch to the requirement, `SC-12` and the feature-set line ("a $1 floor tier from $0" becomes "a lowest tier from $0") is a clarification of an anchor, not a new anchor, so QA1 and Dev are not rerun | A $1 floor fixed in the requirement and its validator (B): the first tier could not be retuned without a delta, against the first goal |
| Q14 | Do `auto-bidding`, `bidding-history`, `auction`, `account-record` and `grade10-admin/auction/internal-bid` get a MODIFIED delta in this change, because their scenarios and worked examples quote amounts that follow the old six tiers? | The product manager's own answer: no. Only the tests and fixtures in the application repository are rewritten. Those specs' worked amounts are read as examples under a different set of configuration values, so they do not contradict the configured schedule; `tech-design.md` lists them. Proposal Modified Capabilities stays at one | A MODIFIED delta for each of the five that restates its amounts (the round's recommended A): Modified Capabilities would grow from one to five and acceptance would wait on five more deltas |
| Q15 | How does this change sit beside the accepted open change `usd-lot-currency-winner-settlement-choice`, which also modifies `grade10-site/auction/bid-increments` (the schedule requirement, the minimum requirement, scenarios `SC-01` to `SC-05`, `SC-07` and `SC-12`) and adds "Every lot is bid in USD" with `SC-13` and `SC-14`? | Ours `depends_on` theirs, so theirs archives first and our MODIFIED blocks state against its folded text. Ours owns the rule-and-configuration text of "Grade10 owns the supported-currency schedules" and "The minimum uses the amount being beaten"; theirs owns the ADDED "Every lot is bid in USD" requirement and the USD-only settlement content. Our blocks copy their text where they changed it and stay value-free, and ours carries no literal tier table. Where both carry a scenario or case id with rewritten text, ours bumps `rev` above theirs. The cluster sheet is `reconciliation.md` in their change directory. The product manager asked for this order | Archiving ours first or neither depending on the other: two changes would restate one requirement with different text, and the archive of the second would overwrite the first. Merging the two changes: each keeps its own scope, acceptance and verdict |
| Q16 | May a listing's starting price carry cents? | The product manager's own answer, in their words: "A: the starting price is whole dollars too." A starting price is a whole-dollar amount, the same rule as bids, maxima and increments. It stays stored as integer minor units, a multiple of one major unit taken from the USD exponent of `@grade10/utils/money`. A starting price with cents is refused, naming the rule, and the listing is unchanged. The rule applies to every path that sets a starting price, a consigned create included (Q19), and to none that is already stored (Q18). Source: the product manager's answer, and Q9. Revised after Q18 and Q19: this row first said the opening minimum is always one a collector can bid. That is withdrawn, because a price stored with cents survives (Q18), so the opening minimum on such a row is held in Raised | Cents allowed, with the opening minimum reading the next whole dollar at or above the starting price (the round's option B): the first bid would stand above the price the operator set and the page, which shows the starting price, would disagree with the minimum. It lost because the product manager wants every amount a collector meets in whole dollars |
| Q17 | Does the admin capability `grade10-admin/auction/listing` carry the whole-dollar starting-price rule, as a MODIFIED delta beside bid-increments? | The product manager's own answer: "1A". Yes: a MODIFIED delta on "Prices and window are writable before publish" states that an operator's draft save, create or update of a starting price with cents is refused, naming the rule, and the listing is unchanged. Proposal Modified Capabilities is now two. The delta copies the text of `usd-lot-currency-winner-settlement-choice`, which also modifies that requirement and archives first (`depends_on`, Q15), and states no value. Source: the product manager's answer, Q16 | No admin delta, the rule living only in bid-increments: the operator's own capability would not state the refusal the operator meets, and its suite would hold no case for it |
| Q18 | What happens to existing stored listings whose starting price carries cents? | The product manager's own answer: "2 leave them". They are left as they are: no migration, no rounding and no rule applied to a stored row. Task 3.5 stays as a count for information only, which needs no decision, and the count is no gate on deploy. Source: the product manager's answer | A one-time migration that rounds stored prices, or applying the rule to a stored row on its next write: either moves a price an operator and, once open, a collector already saw |
| Q19 | Does the whole-dollar starting-price rule apply to a consigned listing, whose starting price comes from the consignment ask? | The product manager's own answer, a correction of their first answer, in their words: "consigned should be whole as well". Yes: Q16 applies to the consigned create path with no exemption, so an ask with cents is refused at create like any other save, naming the rule, and no listing is made. Consignment owns that path ([`add-consignment`](../add-consignment/proposal.md), its lifecycle capability, where the floor alone becomes the lot's start price); it has no durable requirement yet, so this change states the rule in bid-increments and edits no consignment artifact. How the refusal reaches the staff member who agreed the floor is held in Raised. Source: the product manager's answer, Q16 | Consigned listings keeping a starting price with cents from the ask, the product manager's first answer "3 consigned accept cents": it left cents on a lot Grade10 creates itself, and the correction replaced it |
